# M2-5 limitations

What this spike does not establish. Read this before quoting any number from
`measurements.md` in a decision.

## The corpus is synthetic, and small

25 pages, all generated by `scripts/research-m2-5-fixtures.mjs`. No customer
drawing and no real project file was used. That was the brief, and it is the
right call for a spike, but it bounds every result:

- **Glyphs are clean.** The "scanned" pages are rasterised vector text, not
  photographs of paper. They have no skew, no speckle, no fold, no scanner
  gamma, no stamp bleeding into a field, no handwriting. OCR accuracy measured
  here is an upper bound and probably a generous one.
- **Two title-block layouts.** A real office has more, and they vary between
  the consultants on one project.
- **Two pages have a fixed-size title block.** Enough to prove the two scaling
  conventions pull in opposite directions; nowhere near enough to say how often
  either occurs.
- **One page mixes native and OCR sources.** The entire argument for field-level
  source selection rests on that one page -- on the aggregate the page-level
  policy is only two fields behind.
- **The numbering is regular by construction.** Real sets have suffixes,
  revisions in the number, split packages and consultants who number their own
  way.

A measurement on 25 pages that says "0 false positives" means the check did not
fire on 25 synthetic pages. It does not mean the check is safe.

## `templateFits()` is not a guard

The prototype's fit check returns true for all 25 pages, including the ones
where the template reads nothing at all, because A-series sheets share an
aspect ratio. It cannot distinguish "this template applies" from "this template
is about to produce four empty fields".

Anything built on this must not use it as a gate. This is why the architecture
asks the user to confirm placement per sheet size rather than trusting a fit
score.

## Confidence is not a probability

The number Tesseract reports is its own score for its own output. It is not
calibrated against ground truth, not comparable between engines or modes, and
not a probability that a value is correct. In this design it orders a queue.
Any UI text that implies otherwise is a defect.

The review-burden figures in §9 have the same limit, and they got worse when
the corpus grew: **one row is wrong and carries no flag at all** (page 25, a
rotated scanned sheet whose revision OCR'd confidently and incorrectly). An
earlier version of this document said that number was zero. It was zero because
the corpus did not yet contain a page that could produce it.

That is the honest shape of the flagging: it catches low confidence, and a
confidently wrong read is exactly what it cannot catch.

## Preprocessing: a null result, not a negative one

Grayscale and contrast stretching made no measurable difference here. On clean
synthetic glyphs that is what you would expect whether or not preprocessing
helps real scans. Nothing was learned about real scans, and the decision is
deferred rather than settled.

## Timings are from one machine, one run

The end-to-end figures (1.6–3.6 s for 25 pages) come from this machine, with
a warm worker, on this corpus. They are useful for comparing the four policies
against each other, which is what they were taken for. They are not a
throughput estimate, and nothing here measures a 200-page set, memory over a
long run, or what happens when several large sheets are processed in sequence.

Region rendering was measured as pixel and byte counts, which are exact. The
*time* to rasterise a large sheet was not measured at volume.

## The Excel verification covers one application, one version

`measurements.md` §11 is Microsoft Excel 16.0, on Windows, on this machine,
through COM. Not LibreOffice, not Google Sheets, not Numbers, not Excel on
macOS, and not the Excel web client. The formula-evaluation behaviour is likely
similar across them; the leading-zero and text-prefix behaviour may not be, and
neither was checked.

The XLSX result — 12 of 12 values intact — is a statement about that one
application opening a file written by the writer already in this repository.

## What was not built at all

- No title-block detector.
- No layout classifier.
- No production UI. The review queue is a sorted list in a script, not a
  screen, and the cost of reviewing 5 flagged rows out of 25 in a real
  interface is unmeasured.
- No page-range or multi-document handling.
- No persistence of a template between sessions.
- No extraction of anything beyond the four fields.
- No measurement of union word-attribution on a *tight* title block. Union OCR
  places every recognised word inside a field rectangle on this corpus (0
  unplaced), but these fields are well separated. A block whose cells nearly
  touch could put a word on the wrong side of a boundary, and nothing here
  measures how close is too close.
- No handling of a page whose title block is rotated relative to the sheet
  (as opposed to the sheet being rotated, which is covered).

## What was deliberately not attempted

External OCR services, AI APIs and cloud conversion were excluded by the brief.
No measurement was taken for any of them, so this spike says nothing about
whether they would be more accurate — only that the design here does not use
them, and that the probes assert zero external requests rather than claiming it
in prose.
